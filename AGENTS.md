## Prior Art Directive (David, 2026-09-06 — binding on every seat)

Any algorithm, selection rule, data structure, proof technique, or
optimization you implement or propose MUST be annotated with the prior
art you know of: paper/system/author and year, and what you took from
it versus what is yours. Put it in three places: (1) a comment at the
code site, (2) the ledger entry, (3) the final report under a "Prior
art" heading. If you know of none, write "no prior art known to me"
explicitly — silence is not allowed. If you cannot verify (no network
in the sandbox), write "unverified — lead to check" and name the
search terms. Re-deriving a known idea is fine and often the right
move; presenting it without the annotation is a reporting failure.
The lead checks the literature through the proxy and adds citations;
your job is to make the check possible. Receipt for why this exists:
APA-SP1 reproduced the BLASST (arXiv 2512.12087) running-max criterion
without citing it — see /mnt/Shared/APA_SP_Prior_Art_Comparison_2026-09-06.md.
