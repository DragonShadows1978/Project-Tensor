## 1. Public convolution backward

The Python signatures use the same geometry keywords and defaults as the forwards. Each adds `needs=(True, True, True)` and returns `(dx, dw, db)`. The explicit pullback requires compact fp32 TensorCUDA tensors and returns fresh detached tensors, with `None` for disabled slots. Bias is implicit: dB sums the upstream over batch and output time, including output positions with only a bias contribution. The inference forwards and private backward also accept compatible CUDA DLPack producers. The private call takes preallocated destinations and scratch. The wrapper manages those allocations, marks each output modified, and leaves the existing tracked path in place. [Public wrapper](../../tc_signal/tc_signal/_conv_backward.py) · [Forward and tracked entry points](../../tc_signal/tc_signal/__init__.py)

For any native-planned geometry, a convolution uses the channel group that owns the output channel and connects an output time `j` and tap `k` to input time `j*s - pb + k*d`. Transpose convolution uses that affine map from input time to output time. The native dX gather inverts the first map or uses the second directly. Both dW branches reduce `x*dy` over the corresponding in-range pairs, preserving each layout's weight order. Right padding and output padding enter through the resolved extent. The transpose native rule is `output_padding < max(stride, dilation)`. Source inspection covers the tree and tiled GEMM paths. An independent NumPy scatter formulation matched the fp64 helpers and centered differences across 99 Conv and 264 ConvTranspose cases, including 48 dilation-dominant output-padding cases. The maximum absolute directional difference was `1.68e-9`. That CPU check verifies the algebra. GPU arithmetic was exercised on the 60 public GPU cases documented in section 7. [Native kernels](../../tc_signal/src/conv_backward.cu) · [Native plans](../../tc_signal/src/bindings.cpp) · [CPU boundary probes](checks/source_probes_retry.log)

The order of Python validation is reasonable for diagnostic reachability with CUDA hidden: type, dtype, rank/extents, attributes and output shape, mask, then device. The native run repeats geometry and validates DLPack shape, device, strides, and disjoint output/scratch. Two gaps matter for a public contract. The all-off mask skips native limits, which I exercised with `stride=2**63` and a length-one input. Active masks require the operands' CUDA device to be current because of the allocation path. The existing tracked path shares that limitation. [F05 and F10](findings.json)

## 3. Amendments and cotangent census

**A1-1 is sound for the registered backward comparison.** It compares two VJPs on the same MFCC cotangent at the deployed fp32 wave, up to the candidate cast. The reference derives the cotangent independently of the candidate's supplied upstream, so candidate-only sign/drop faults remain visible. The Voice MFCC implementation is shared by both arms, and those gates inherit its correctness. On the real wave the cast moved the worst row by 0.003617 of its bar. Moving the cotangent from the fp64 wave to the captured wave moved the style reference by 0.5875 of its bar. [Fresh crop reference](checks/fp64_crop_retry.log)

**A defect can survive the probed cotangents.** Take an identity forward in three coordinates, linear probe `r=e1`, and loss `L(y)=y2 + y3^2/2`. If the captured wave has third coordinate zero and the fp64 wave has third coordinate ε, the two MFCC analogues are `g_c=e2` and `g_64=e2+ε e3`. A broken pullback `diag(1,1,2) u` agrees on `r` and `g_c`, yet errs by `ε e3` on `g_64`. On the real crop I constructed a unit `q` orthogonal to the linear and cast MFCC upstreams by projecting the fp64-wave upstream off their span. A rank-one error `v*(qᵀu)` has negligible projections on both test upstreams (absolute `1.08e-13` and `2.24e-9` in this computation), a projection of 0.004382 on the original fp64-wave cotangent, and unit response on `q`. The gate is sensitive to errors on the measured upstreams and leaves other VJP directions untested. [Constructed real-wave example](checks/fp64_crop_retry.log)

**A2-1 uses independent forward finite differences.** The fp64 reference gradient selects or filters probes. I also evaluated the nonlinear MFCC loss on the same waveform changes recentered at the captured wave and got 0.956364052650. This extra check exercises the shared MFCC derivative along that direction. The nonlinear fp64-wave slope on that actual A2-1 direction was 0.956353232654. ## 5. Ten-second memory measurement

The reader queries NVML's per-device process lists, selects its own PID, sums the reported per-device bytes, and samples around all marked phases. It raises on explicit NVML errors and an unavailable byte count. The peak recomputed from the valid checkpoint series was **3,030,384,640 B**. All 349 entries had a memory value. There were 27 forward, 30 loss, 283 backward and 8 collection samples, plus one before the first phase mark. The median was 2.217 ms, with 71 gaps over 5 ms. The longest ran from 9.16 ms before the forward mark to 2.107 s after it. Timestamps are taken at query start, so phase attribution is approximate. The R4a pool high-water plus observed nonpool basis was 3,510,829,056 B and uses device-wide nonpool boundary samples. The plain arm's observed peak was 9,539,944,448 B. [Raw-series recount](checks/recount_retry.log)

The median suffices to pass the programmed test. A 2.116 s blind interval leaves room for an unobserved transient. Run coverage and a high-water cross-check are needed for the hard memory claim. The digest patch changes instrumentation only when applied, but its lifetime ends at the return from the mirrored forward. The replay closure then calls both restored digest functions. I exercised the actual closure with marker functions and observed `off` on forward and both functions called on replay. Forward and backward timing are consequently for different instrumentation modes. The nominal recorded phases were 2.335 s forward, 0.073 s loss and 1.586 s backward. The reviewer did not rerun a GPU line. [Digest scope probe](checks/source_probes_retry.log) · [F01 and F04](findings.json)

## 6. Ranking eligibility and structural zeros

The ONNX graph check independently found exactly 29 trained biases whose convolution output has just one consumer, the normalized input of InstanceNormalization. A constant per-channel shift cancels when the norm subtracts the time mean, so the derivative with respect to that shift is zero in real arithmetic. The two tail parameters remain eligible and have nonzero gradients. The separate 12 identity-norm initializers resolve to six all-ones scales and six all-zero biases. They can have nonzero gradients and their exclusion follows Voice's policy to keep those shared identity-affine values fixed. The two sets are disjoint. The ranking receipt has 274 eligible of 315 distinct trained entries and 53,241,978 eligible elements. The style vector is compared by G1 and kept outside the weight-ranking list. [Graph census](checks/fp64_crop_retry.log) · [Ranking recount](checks/recount_retry.log)

A1-3's threshold handles floating-point cancellation. On the fresh crop the largest excluded bias was `1.32e-17` of the largest linear row and `9.82e-18` of the largest captured-wave MFCC row. The smallest outside row divided by the largest inside row was `1.91e12` and `5.42e12`, respectively. Both pass the 1e-12 fraction and 1e6 gap thresholds. The numerical G1 logic should accept exact zeros in that structural class as well. [Zero-gap check](checks/fp64_crop_retry.log) · [F11](findings.json)

## Prior art

- Reverse accumulation, Linnainmaa (1970), and checkpoint rematerialization, Chen et al. (2016), underlie the inspected VJP and replay. This review's contribution is the replay-scope check and the same-cotangent acceptance analysis.
- Dumoulin and Visin (2016) describe convolution arithmetic and its transpose. Higham (2002) describes centered differences and step error. The review's scatter witness, dilation/padding coverage and recentered MFCC finite-difference check apply those ideas to the current operands.
- Davis and Mermelstein (1980) supply the MFCC feature family. Griffin and Lim (1984) and Kaneko et al. (2022) provide STFT and iSTFTNet context. Voice supplies the application-specific reference, VJP and alignment.
- The orthogonal projection and rank-one construction use standard linear algebra as in Golub and Van Loan (2013). The particular undetected perturbation uses this round's real cotangents.
- NVIDIA NVML documents the per-process query. NVIDIA CUDA documents pool high-water counters. DeMillo, Lipton and Sayward (1978) describe mutation testing. This review independently checked the actual sample series and mutation traces.
- Unverified — lead to check these author/year and method terms. For this project's particular run/operand SHA join and exact probe inventory proposal, no additional prior art known to me.
